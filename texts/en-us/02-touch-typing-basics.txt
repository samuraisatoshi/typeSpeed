Touch Typing Basics

Touch typing relies on placing your fingers on a fixed starting
position, known as the home row. On a QWERTY keyboard the left
hand rests on A, S, D and F, while the right hand rests on J, K,
L and semicolon. Small raised bumps on the F and J keys help you
find this position without looking down.

Each finger is responsible for a specific group of keys nearby
its home row position. The index fingers cover the most keys,
reaching up and down as well as sideways to neighboring columns,
while the pinky fingers usually handle punctuation, shift and
the outer edges of the keyboard.

Good posture matters as much as finger placement. Keep your
wrists straight, your elbows close to your body, and your eyes
on the screen instead of the keyboard. Typing with correct
posture reduces fatigue and lowers the risk of repetitive strain
injuries during long practice sessions.

Speed comes naturally once accuracy is consistent. Beginners
often try to type fast before their fingers know the layout,
which leads to more corrections and a slower overall pace.
Practicing slowly and deliberately, then gradually increasing
speed, produces better long term results than racing from the
start.
